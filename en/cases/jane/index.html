The Reveal

“The jungle doesn't follow rules. Neither does Jane.”

Trouve ta Jane is an experimental fashion identity exploring femininity beyond fixed definitions. The first chapter centres on THE VINE, a sculptural handbag concept built with creative direction, generative AI and manual image editing. A concept project: no physical prototype. The page starts with the result, then goes back to the logo and the process behind it.

The Campaign

Jane does not pose in nature, she owns it. One location, a few micro-zones: mossy roots, black basalt, a grey studio. One rule: every frame has one dominant device, and its neighbours never repeat it.

Beyond the Product

One bag, one box. A deep matte olive rigid box with a sliding tray, burgundy inside, cream tissue, a soft dust bag, a wax seal. Burgundy is the hero colour, olive the structure, cream the soft neutral.

The Identity

Everything on this page grows from one small mark. The brand began as a logo, and every part of it carries an idea.

The name is not about a woman to be found, but a woman who finds herself. The serif is elegant, with a jungle atmosphere and a touch of romance. The handwritten “ta” is organic and adventurous, and it recalls vines. The letters sit very close together, like people drawing closer. The hand-drawn sign is a heart and a tropical leaf at once.

The handwritten letters, close to vines, became the first hint for the intertwined handles. The sign became a mark of its own, on hardware and packaging.

The full name lives inside and on the packaging, small and tone on tone: a large logo on the bag would compete with the folds and the straps.

From Nature to Form

Vines don't follow a plan. They twist, overlap and find their own way. That became the design language: two tubular leather handles that can be worn parallel or intertwined.

One Bag, Multiple Forms

I wanted one stylish bag that is also comfortable. Backpacks are comfortable, but good-looking ones are hard to find, and stylish one-shoulder bags tire the shoulder under load. THE VINE is my visual answer: four straps, three ways to carry.

Designing with AI

Generating a good image was the easy part. The hard part was keeping one bag, one woman and one world consistent. Here are four places where the generator broke the design, and what I changed.

Bag and straps

Problem

In one version the bag itself turned into a different bag.

Before

Method

Fixed by sending the bag as three sketch views and replacing it in the approved frame.

Replace the bag in image 1 with the bag from images 2–4 (three-quarter, front/back and side views). Keep the scene, lighting and composition.

After

Materials: moss

Problem

The moss turned into plastic clumps.

Before

Method

Fixed with a text-only prompt: the moss redescribed as one dense, matte layer.

Make the moss deep, dark forest green: one dense, velvety, matte layer with a fine texture. No tufts, clumps or pom-poms; not bright or saturated.

After

Materials: basalt

Problem

The basalt got flower-like patterns instead of a natural grain.

Before

Method

Fixed with a text-only prompt that kept every crack and changed only the texture.

Change only the texture of the basalt ground: fine-grained, dark charcoal, matte. Keep every crack, edge and shape exactly. No new cracks or stones; remove the flower-like patterns.

After

The heroine

Problem

Wrong face, wrong earring, and two ordinary rings on two fingers instead of our one ring.

Before

Method

Face and earring replaced from a reference portrait; the two rings replaced by one, from a photo of the ring.

Replace the model's face and earring in image 1 with those from image 2. Keep everything else. Replace the two rings in image 1 with one ring on the ring finger, as in image 2.

After

Gravity and contact

Problem

The bag floated and tilted above the box, and an extra strap appeared: two errors in one frame.

Before

Method

Two separate text prompts: one placed the bag flat in the box, one fixed the strap layout.

Place the bag fully inside the box, flat on its back. Level and straight: no tilt, no floating. Keep the bag design unchanged. Fix only the straps: the two upper straps lie flat under the closure tab, the two side straps stay at the sides. Four separate straps, no crossing. Do not redesign the bag.

After

  • Lighting design

    Before lighting adjectives.

  • Product identity

    Without composition copying.

  • One change at a time

    With the approved frame as master.

  • Other errors

    function killed the character (the bag became stock, relief and asymmetry brought it back); the backpack view (the generator saw an ordinary backpack, so the pose was placed by hand); flat lighting (the light was designed as shapes before mood); reference copying (a reference gave identity, composition came from text); bag swaps (one image gives structure, another design, a third the setting).

Outcome and Learnings

The project brings together a recognisable conceptual product, several carrying configurations, a campaign, product photography and a packaging identity. The goal was not to remove the unpredictability of generative AI but to turn experimentation into a more intentional process.

  • What worked

    A clear reference hierarchy, one change at a time, hand edits where the generator fails.

  • What remains

    This is a concept, with no prototype and no ergonomic tests. THE VINE is chapter 01 of a collection; Seed and Root stay concepts.
